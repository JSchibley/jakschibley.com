Making Keyboard Circuit Boards at Home

James and I have been making custom gaming keyboards with circuit boards milled on a small desktop CNC. The question behind the project is simple: can we make the boards at home and keep the development flow going?

From printed traces back to copper

The conductive-filament controllers from our fighting game controller project worked well for reading keys. But we couldn’t get I2C signals, used for communication between components, through the printed traces to drive status screens or build split keyboards.

So we came back to copper. A printed circuit board, or PCB, starts here as a copper-clad board. The CNC removes just enough copper to electrically separate the traces, leaving the connections the circuit needs.

From design to board

James designs the boards in KiCad. We use pcb2gcode to turn those designs into toolpaths for a Genmitsu Cubiko desktop CNC. The machine then mills the traces, drills the component holes, and cuts out the board.

That gives us a physical board to examine and improve, with the design and manufacturing steps happening at home.

What we’re learning

The idea is straightforward. Getting consistently good results has given us plenty to work through:

  • Keeping the board flat. We’re cutting through a thin copper layer, so small differences in surface height matter.
  • Balancing depth, feed rate, and bit size. We’ve had copper burrs, fragile little islands, and a broken bit along the way.
  • Designing for our process. Trace widths, clearances, and component pads need to work with our cutters and with assembly at home.

Each iteration mixes changes to the board design, changes to the toolpaths, and a close look at what the machine actually cut. A design can look reasonable on a screen and still leave a tiny strip of copper where we don’t want one.

Still in progress

We’re still tuning the milling process. As with the eBird database and conductive-filament projects, the appeal is shortening the distance between an idea and useful feedback. Every board gives us something concrete to examine and improve.
