About

I’m Jak Schibley. I’ve spent much of my career behind the scenes, keeping systems running and helping people figure out what to do when they don’t.

I retired in October 2025. These days I’m working on bird research databases, keyboards, and whatever else catches my interest.

Consulting

I ran a consulting practice called Innovative Data, working with companies in manufacturing, logistics, and operations. The work varied: adding manufacturing information to CAD systems, using sales data to anticipate inventory needs, improving truck utilization, and contributing to robotic systems that tested gas meters for leaks.

Much of it involved understanding how people were already doing their work, then figuring out where software could help.

PubTV

I founded PubTV and created its technology.

Nielsen’s data and tools were built for networks with a common national schedule. Public television needed something different. We combined Nielsen peoplemeter data with Tribune Media scheduling information and built tools for clients with different levels of data expertise.

There were standard reports for routine questions. For people who wanted to explore further, there were summaries they could tune by adjusting filters and parameters. For custom research, we provided training and consulting so people could run their own queries against the databases.

PubTV was later acquired by Trac Media.

Writely

Around the time of PubTV’s acquisition, I worked with Writely, the online word processor that became Google Docs.

I designed and implemented infrastructure across multiple locations, with redundancy and security built in. I helped it handle traffic spikes as it grew, then supported the move to Google’s infrastructure after the acquisition.

Google

At Google, I worked on infrastructure and became someone teams called during serious incidents.

I understood the systems well enough to know what we could change without breaking something else, and I knew how to find the experts we needed. I was particularly good at getting everyone working from the same understanding of what was happening, what had been tried, and what we were doing next.

Some of my work included:

  • A machine delivery system that processed millions of machines and configured their roles in Google’s datacenters.
  • Storage upgrade automation that increased annual upgrade volume from hundreds of petabytes to tens of zettabytes. Supply-chain constraints limited how many new storage machines we could build, so we upgraded the existing ones at a scale that kept us ahead of demand.
  • Abnormality detection and uptime measurement across different products, with results presented quarterly across Google.

Patent

I also worked on identifying separate conversations when several people were talking at once, using audio alone.

That led to “Turn-Taking Patterns for Conversation Identification” (US20140081637A1), which explores how to take a roomful of overlapping conversations, identify how many conversations are happening, and determine which speakers are participating in each.

Now

One thing I like about retirement is the break from having my projects dictated by entropy.

I get to pick what to work on and have a little time to mix in some aesthetics. That includes building an eBird research database for my wife Lisa’s work, making controllers and keyboards with my son James, and recreating the ThinkPad 701C’s butterfly keyboard mechanism.

The projects section shows how those are going.
